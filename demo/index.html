Flight route weather prediction

Weather at any point along a flight — before the aircraft gets there.

A neural ODE learns how temperature, wind, pressure and visibility evolve through space and time over Canada, then predicts conditions at any latitude, longitude, altitude and minute — not just where a station happens to sit.

Evaluation results

Scored against held-out ground truth on two full test routes (18,581 and 18,110 points). These are the real numbers the model above was judged on — including where it still struggles.

VariableRouteMAERMSER²Read

Pressure is the standout (R² ≈ 0.999)

Near-perfect across both routes — the model has clearly learned the vertical structure of the atmosphere, which is exactly what altitude-dependent flight planning needs most.

Wind speed holds up well (R² ≈ 0.85)

MAE around 7 m/s is in a useful range for fuel-burn and groundspeed planning, and performance is consistent between both test routes.

Temperature is directionally right, not precise (R² ≈ 0.5)

MAE ~8°C is usable for icing checks but not fine-grained performance calculations — there's real room to improve here.

Visibility doesn't work yet (negative R²)

The model collapses toward near-constant predictions. Visibility is notoriously hard to model from station data alone; it would need a dedicated fog/cloud-aware module, not a tweak.
